# Competitor positioning

Internal. Public information only, read 2026-10-05 from Chrome Web Store listings and public review
summaries (sources at the end). Figures move; recheck before quoting any of them publicly, and never
use competitor names, logos or claims in Senuma marketing (MESSAGING_SYSTEM.md rules).

## 1. The field

Two families meet on the new tab:

- **Beautiful new tabs:** Momentum, Bonjourr, Tabliss, Infinity New Tab. Looks, calm, a few widgets.
- **Workspace / tab organizers:** Toby, Workona, start.me. Organize work; usually accounts, sync
  and paid plans.

Senuma sits between them: a workspace on the new tab that is also personal-looking and private.

What reviews say, in short:

- **Momentum:** loved for looks and focus; complaints about paying for basics such as one custom image.
- **Toby:** loved for organization; frustration with the free-tier cap and per-workspace billing.
- **Workona:** powerful but a work product with accounts and teams; price is the main objection.
- **Bonjourr / Tabliss:** top ratings for privacy, simplicity and openness; light on organization.

## 3. Gaps Senuma can own

1. **Organization without an account or a paywall.** Toby and Workona users who hit limits need a
   free place for their daily tools. Senuma: unlimited Spaces, Modes, links; nothing gated.
2. **Modes that change the whole page.** Contexts exist elsewhere as lists of tabs; none switches
   Spaces, look, search engine and dock together.
3. **Search that knows your own sites.** One box for the web and your links, with shortcuts and
   custom engines, plus a command center. Beautiful tabs have a plain search box; organizers
   search only their own items.
4. **Your own photo, finely tuned, for free, never uploaded.** Momentum charges for custom images;
   Senuma stores them on the device with fit, position, dim, blur and atmosphere.
5. **Privacy as a fact, not a promise.** No account, no analytics, optional permissions released
   after use. Bonjourr-level privacy with Workona-level organization.
6. **Native Turkish** with a Turkish guide, from day one (alongside English).

Where Senuma is weaker today (be honest in copy and roadmap): no sync between computers (backup file
only), no saving of open tab sessions, no widgets (weather, to-dos, notes), not open source,
no reviews yet, English + Turkish only.

## 4. Positioning

**Category:** Personal Browser Workspace.

**Statement:** For people whose day runs through the browser, Senuma turns every new tab into
their own workspace: the sites they use organized into Spaces, one search bar that knows them,
Modes for each part of the day, and a look that is theirs, private by design, without an account.

**Core combination (validated):** Organization + Search + Modes + Customization + Privacy. Each
competitor owns one or two of these; none owns all five. Keep the order: organization and search
are the reason to stay; customization is the reason to try; privacy is the reason to trust.

**Refinements:**

- Lead with *use*, show *beauty*: headline benefits are organization and speed; visuals carry the
  look. (Momentum/Bonjourr already own “beautiful new tab”.)
- Say “workspace”, never “tab manager”: Senuma does not save tab sessions.
- “Free, no account” goes high on the page; it answers the main objection to organizers.
- Do not claim speed numbers, user counts or rankings until measured (MESSAGING_SYSTEM.md rules).

## 5. Store presentation lessons

- Top listings show the product in use in the first screenshot and keep captions short.
- High ratings come from many reviews: a respectful, well-timed review request matters
  (HELP_FEEDBACK_SYSTEM.md § 5). Chrome's rating now favours recent reviews, and the “Featured”
  badge is being retired in 2026, so steady recent reviews and relevance matter more than badges.
- Ranking uses quality, relevance of name and description, and ratings: keep the description
  specific (Spaces, Modes, search shortcuts, Ctrl+K, backgrounds, privacy).
