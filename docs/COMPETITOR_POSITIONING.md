## 1. The field

Three families meet on the new tab:

- **Beautiful new tabs:** Momentum, Bonjourr, Tabliss, Infinity New Tab. Looks, calm, widgets.
- **Workspace / tab organizers:** Toby, Workona, start.me. Organize work; accounts, sync, paid plans.
- **Feed new tabs for a profession:** daily.dev (developer news on every new tab).

Also competing for the same slot without being extensions: Chrome's own tab groups and
bookmarks bar, and browsers with built-in workspaces. They set the baseline people compare with.

Senuma sits between the first two: a workspace on the new tab that is also personal-looking.

What reviews and listings say, in short:

- **Momentum:** loved for looks and focus; recurring complaint about paying for basics such as one
  custom image.
- **Toby:** loved for organization; frustration with the free cap and per-workspace billing.
- **Workona:** powerful, but a work product with accounts; price is the objection; the extension
  itself was last updated in January 2025.
- **Bonjourr:** the category's best rating; privacy, simplicity, 39 languages; light on organization.
- **Tabliss:** liked, but English-only and not updated since 2022.
- **Infinity:** broad feature list (sync, mail, to-dos, notes); 33 languages.
- **daily.dev:** owns “developer new tab” as a news feed, not as a workspace; it competes for the
  primary persona's new tab even though it solves a different problem.

## 3. Where Senuma can clearly win

1. **Organization without an account or a paywall.** People who hit Toby's or Workona's limits
   need a free place for their daily tools: unlimited Spaces, Modes and links, nothing gated.
2. **Modes that change the whole page.** Elsewhere a context is a list of tabs or a page of links;
   none switches Spaces, look, search engine and dock together.
3. **Search that knows your own sites.** One box for the web and your links, with shortcuts,
   custom engines and a command center. Beautiful tabs have a plain box; organizers search only
   their own items.
4. **Your own photo, finely tuned, free, kept on the device.** A paid extra elsewhere.
5. **Stated plainly:** no Senuma account, no analytics, no Senuma cloud required in this release,
   workspace data local; and the product says what does go out (searches, site icons).
6. **Help inside the product** (2.0.2): an offline guide and contextual links; competitors send
   people to a website.
7. **Native Turkish**, interface and guide, from the start.
8. **For developers, a workspace rather than a feed:** the developer persona's alternative is a
   news feed on every tab; Senuma offers their tools and a keyboard instead.

Where Senuma is weaker today (keep copy and roadmap honest): no sync between computers, no saved
tab sessions, no widgets (weather, to-dos, notes, feed), two languages against 30–39 for the
largest products, no reviews yet, source visible but not open source.

## 4. Positioning

**Category:** Personal Browser Workspace.

**Statement:** For people whose day runs through the browser, Senuma turns every new tab into
their own workspace: the sites they use organized into Spaces, one search bar that knows them,
Modes for each part of the day, and a look that is theirs; free, without a Senuma account.

**Core combination (holds after the refresh):** Organization + Search + Modes + Customization +
Privacy. Each competitor owns one or two; none owns all five. Organization and search are the
reason to stay; customization is the reason to try; privacy is the reason to trust.

**Refinements:**

- Lead with use, show beauty (the “beautiful new tab” slot is taken).
- Say “workspace”, never “tab manager”: Senuma does not save tab sessions.
- “Free, no Senuma account” goes high: it answers the main objection to organizers.
- Languages are a real gap against the leaders: the localization order (Spanish, French, then
  German, Portuguese) matters for reach, after the launch proves the product.
- No speed numbers, user counts or rankings until measured.

## 5. Store presentation lessons

- Top listings show the product in use in the first screenshot with a short caption.
- Ratings count: Chrome now weighs recent reviews more, and the “Featured” badge is being retired
  in 2026, so steady recent reviews and a relevant description matter more than badges.
- Two established products have not updated their extension in over a year; an actively
  maintained listing with visible update notes is itself a signal.
